## B5 — colleague report review

Decision: do not approve for printing. Problems, in order of consequence:

1. The reaction is only 96 N for 120 N applied: a 24 N (20%) imbalance, not close to the 1% check. Resolve the load, restraints, reaction components, all bodies and any unconstrained modes before using the results.
2. Peak von Mises 41 MPa gives 45/41 = 1.10; it cannot be dismissed because it occurs at a mounting-hole edge. The report does not establish any adequate safety margin or give a pass requirement. Distinguish a restraint singularity from real bearing and ligament stress, report the excluded peak, evaluate a distance plateau, and repeat with a physically justified washer/head-bearing restraint.
3. The reported mesh series is not converged: 37.6 to 41.0 MPa changes 9.0% relative to the coarse result (8.3% relative to the fine), greater than 5%; only two sizes are reported. A hole edge can be singular and will not necessarily converge under global refinement. Use at least three meshes, a constant physical exclusion or representative radius, local checks, and converge the governing failure measures.
4. For layers stacked along Z, sigma-x is an in-plane normal stress, not tension across the layers. Extract sigma-z tension with its location and compare with 22.5 MPa, and calculate shear traction on the layer plane against 13 MPa. Even if 19.5 MPa were cross-layer tension, the factor would be 22.5/19.5 = 1.15, not 2.3. Locate and converge both measures; compare credible print directions.
5. The fine mesh had 520,000 nodes, above the playbook's 450,000 maximum and this benchmark's 200,000-node cap; running a large solve beside another heavy solve was not justified. Re-run under the applicable per-solve and machine-wide limits, with targeted refinement.
6. The STL comparison lacks units and cannot be dismissed as rounding. If 1.02 and 16,700 are both mm³ they differ enormously; 1.02 in³ is about 16,715 mm³, however, so an inch/mm mismatch is a plausible explanation. Establish the actual STL units and frame, check its volume in mm³ within 0.5%, bounds, watertightness and one shell, and re-export if needed.
7. The hand calculation assumes the 40 mm dimension is the bending depth. Its arithmetic (I = 21,333 mm⁴ and 3.375 MPa) is correct for that assumed axis, but the report does not establish that axis or the load path. If the 4 mm thickness is the bending depth instead, I = 213.3 mm⁴ and stress is 33.75 MPa. Draw the frame and section, identify load direction and actual bending axis, then compare stress at a plain section against the appropriate hand estimate.
8. There is no meaningful displacement check: 0.9 mm has no hand prediction, location, direction, or interpretation. Compute expected displacement for the actual support and load and compare.
9. All four hole walls fixed can suppress slip and local deformation, and creates artificial hole-edge peaks; bolt preload, head/washer bearing, wall contact and realistic constraints are unspecified. Establish the load path and compare a second support model, including fastener loads and plate bearing where relevant.
10. “120 N on the motor face” does not specify how the motor transmits it. If the load enters through fasteners, a rigid or distributed load on a motor face may stiffen the holes or bypass the real path. State the actual bearing faces and distribution, compare an alternative physically representative load and inspect nearby holes separately from load masks.
11. The reaction lacks vector components, applied-load verification, and support-by-support distribution. A correct resultant alone would not establish equilibrium in the intended directions or the fastener load path.
12. The report lacks model identity and hash, material stiffness/Poisson ratio, mesh controls and solver details, stress locations for layer and shear measures, treatment of exclusions, and enough information to reproduce the case. Keep nodal results and a traceable script or study with those details.
13. The print verdict omits print orientation feasibility, wall/clearance and mating checks, print process and infill assumptions, fastener tightening/preload, and the limits of a linear static model. Complete the relevant pre-print review after the analysis is corrected.

Required redo: establish the geometry, load and support; repair equilibrium; validate against the correctly oriented hand calculation; re-run a bounded, locally refined and converged series with alternative support/load checks; evaluate all three failure measures for a chosen orientation; verify the export and print fit. Until then neither the reported peak nor the pass verdict is established.
